## 1. The frameworks he repeats

### TVGP — his master checklist (stated explicitly 2026-08-03)
> "When these 4 things align together. Expect epic outcomes — 1. Theme: a strong theme with
> tailwinds. 2. Growth: Growth catalysts like New products, New segments, Newer margins etc.
> 3. Promoter: Promoters who care for minorities and are hell bent to grow the business.
> 4. Valuations: Peg of less than 1.5x or max 2x in hyper growth stocks. Business entering
> fresh stage 2."

- **Theme** — a real tailwind, ideally a *sunrise* industry ("sunrise is always better than
  sunset"). New sector creation (single-speciality hospitals, space, deep tech) is the richest
  hunting ground because there's no incumbent Mcap yet.
- **Growth** — a *specific* catalyst (new product, new segment, new geography, new margin
  vector), not just "grew 20% last year".
- **Promoter** — a **positive** attribute, not merely the absence of fraud: cares for
  minorities, is hell-bent to grow, is climbing the value-added curve. His 2026-08-29 ICICI-vs-
  HDFC essay is the thesis in full: ICICI's Mcap went 2.31L cr → 10.21L cr in the 8 years after
  Sandeep Bakshi took over, nearly closing a 2.3x gap to HDFC. "Proves the Power of the
  Management in deciding long term outcomes."
- **Valuation** — PEG < 1.5x (max 2x for genuine hypergrowth). "PEG", not "P/E" — cheapness in
  isolation is a trap (see §1 Rate-of-change).

### "Unique businesses" = moats (the 20-Unique-Businesses series)
Filter, stated repeatedly: **high barriers to entry · cannot be replicated even with a blank
cheque · good return ratios · limited competitors · doing something cutting-edge.** Examples he
ran publicly: Inox India, an ASML-type "one company without which AI does not exist", a
chemical co that's "one of only four globally".

### Value-chain PhD + supply-side (his single most distinctive edge)
> "Played the data centre theme by doing PhD on value chain — Substrates, Chips, Optics, Gas
> gensets, Hospitality services for data centre workers, Semiconductor testing & inspection,
> GRID cos in US, Memory chips in US/Korea/Japan, EDA software, EUV machine manufacturer, Solid
> oxide Fuel cells, Cooling solutions." (2026-04-14)

- Map the **whole** chain, then pick the node where **barrier to entry is highest AND growth
  is fastest** (2026-07-24, EV value-chain walk-through: OEMs hyper-competitive → differential
  assemblies consolidated → battery China-dominated → Indian battery-chemicals fortunes
  undecided → advanced plastics → shunts, one Indian player).
- **"Be a student of the supply side rather than demand forecasting"** (2026-07-27). The
  capital cycle: more money → more competition → lower returns → consolidation → *survivors
  print money*. Applied to diagnostics (bearish 2022–24 on new entrants, flipped bullish FY26
  when pricing turned), GLP-1 in India (expects commoditisation — "always study the supply
  side"), bulk chemicals (China anti-involution capacity cuts = bullish; but see peak-margin
  warning below).
- Watch for: capacity being added vs. withdrawn, China shutdowns, competitor entry/exit,
  imports being designed out.

### Rate-of-change of growth > cheapness; "PE isn't valuation"
> "Cheap gives dopamine hit, but trend with a positive rate of change is what makes the real
> money." (2026-04-24) · "Mr Market = Show me the growth and terminal value. Pe doesn't matter
> in isolation and PE isn't valuation." (2026-07-05, on TCS at 14x) · "If the rate of change
> of growth changes from high to moderate. Expect Mr Market to adjust the multiples."
> (2026-07-07, on Trent's 20-quarter deceleration) · "Markets don't have a memory when the
> rate of change occurs." (2026-08-25)

The re-rating driver is the **second derivative** of growth. A great business decelerating
from a high multiple is dangerous; an ugly business inflecting is where the money is.

### Growth traps (the inverse of value traps)
> "Stocks go to 100-150x PE and to move from there they need to report 50-100%+ growth every
> quarter. Which beyond a point becomes mathematically improbable. The moment the growth
> doesn't come — 40-50% correction is the outcome. Many examples from Trent to Kaynes to E2E."
> (2026-07-07)

Companion observation (2026-07-07): "80-150 PE stocks have stopped working" — a whole-cohort
regime shift, sectoral rotation out of the priciest names.

### Earnings quality / peak-margin warning (his most-repeated bear tell)
> "Lot of the chemical companies reporting blockbuster nos are a disaster waiting to happen.
> Inventory gains + Realisation gains are artificially inflating the E in PE. Don't value them
> on peak margins." (2026-08-04) · "careful to bifurcate between volume vs value growth. Many
> would have seen inventory gains and abnormal chemical pricing due to the war." (2026-07-29)

Before trusting a margin/PAT inflection: is it **structural** (mix shift, operating leverage,
VAP) or **cyclical** (realisation, inventory gain, one-off)? Value on normalised, not peak,
margins.

### Pre-opex / operating-leverage setups (his favourite "unique" pattern)
> "Never ignore a business where: 1. Gross margins are expanding 2. Ebitda margins have
> declined due to pre opex 3. Historical track record is stellar 4. Company is doing pain
> today for gain tomorrow. These are unique set ups where investors discard these companies due
> to optically low profitability... these businesses are under earning. Today's pain ends up
> becoming tomorrow's gain when opex phase ends — which is when we get operating leverage."
> (2026-06-02)

### VAP (value-added products) mental model
Take notice the moment a manufacturer starts talking about **VAP mix increasing** — higher
margin, higher barrier. "Many many multibaggers have been made with this mental model."
(2026-06-19). Related: "who's adding *capabilities* instead of just *capacities*" (2026-06-09);
"chasing capabilities vs current ROCE" — companies with ugly ROCE prints quietly building
capabilities the market takes 5–13 years to price (2026-05-30).

### "Speed breaker" buying
> "Occasionally, Mr market gives such opportunities. All one needs is some brains but a big gut
> to stomach the volatility. This is what I call the speed breaker approach to buying stocks.
> One off negative event which won't impair the earnings power." (2026-06-12, on exporters
> punished for tariffs — "differentiated between exporters who are truly unique vs run of the
> mill like textiles. Tracked them, bought them, stayed patient.")

The test is **impairing vs non-impairing**. A regulatory/tariff/war shock that doesn't touch
long-run earnings power on a genuinely unique business = the entry, not a reason to sell.

### "Go where the government is going"
Track policy/liquidity direction as a discovery filter: US Defense Production Act
determinations (grid, transformers, CRGO), Delhi EV policy, US supply-chain reshoring (→ he
initiated on **Intel** "simply following what the government was doing"). (2026-05-06, 2026-05-03)

### Global-first → Indian proxy
The repeatable discovery motion behind much of his book:
> "1. Read an article on FT about Gas turbines demand... 2. Read concalls of SIEMENS energy and
> an interview of Mitsubishi... 3. Identified the Indian beneficiary in GAS Gensets."
> (quoted again 2026-04-22)

To gauge whether AI capex is still accelerating: read **ASML** and **TSMC** concalls (2026-07-18).
Memory: **Samsung / SK Hynix** ("tight market can last well into the 2030s" per SK Hynix CEO).
Power: **Bloom Energy** concall ("chips without power are inventory, not intelligence").

### Mental liquidity / flexibility
> "Ultimate test of mental liquidity as an investor is to re-enter the stocks that you sold as
> the facts change. This is the litmus test." (2026-08-12) · "Why investing requires
> flexibility & change?" — the full diagnostics reversal, bearish → bullish once the capital
> cycle turned (2026-07-27).

### Index churn — "there is no safety in market caps"
Recurring thesis (2026-06-01, 06-06, 08-12, 08-18, 08-19): Nifty 50/500 constituents will
change materially over "a couple of years"; the index is bank/O&G/large-IT heavy (low-teens
growth at best); the new winners are **outside** the index; analogous to US "Nifty Fifty"
1970s → tech. "Safety comes from underlying business and not from MCap." A *large* company can
still be a great multi-year hold on a **specific management or structural inflection** (the
ICICI/Bakshi example) — but "good business" alone is not enough at large size.
