## 6. Revision protocol (runs every 2 days)

This document is regenerated automatically. Each cycle:

1. **Re-check fundamentals**: pull the latest state from `research/scripts/data/portfolio.json` and `valuepickr-screen/data/{screen-ranking.json, conviction-scores.json}` for any conviction changes, new red flags, or newly-qualified Tier-A names.
2. **Re-run the technical scan**: refetch weekly OHLC for every current holding + watchlist name, recompute the 30-week EMA, and check for a crossing since the last cycle.
3. **Apply the EMA discipline mechanically**:
   - A current holding whose **weekly close crosses below its 30W EMA** gets flagged for exit/trim next cycle (one weekly close, not an intraday dip, to avoid whipsaw).
   - A watchlist name that **crosses above its 30W EMA** (fresh signal, not already-extended) becomes eligible to add, funded from the cash buffer.
   - Positions that get very extended (empirically, >40% above the EMA in this pool) are flagged as "don't add here," not sold — the trend is still up.
4. **Check the book against the Section 8 capital-preservation caps** (position size, liquidity/venue, shared-factor clusters, cash floor, governance exclusions) and flag or correct any breach.
5. **Log every change** in the Revision Log below with the date, what changed, and why (fundamental trigger vs. technical trigger vs. risk-cap breach) — never silently overwrite the prior allocation without a stated reason.

## 8. Capital-preservation risk framework (standing rules)

These sit *on top of* the EMA discipline in Section 6. The EMA rules decide direction; these decide how much, and cap the ways a permanent loss can happen. Each scheduled run should check the book against every rule and flag breaches in the Revision Log.

**A. Structural stop (already in force).** Every position's exit is a weekly close below its 30W EMA. The "Dist. to stop" column in Section 2 is therefore each name's built-in maximum loss before the systematic exit (gap risk aside). No discretionary "give it room" overrides.

**B. Position-size caps.**
- No single equity position above **12%** of capital at entry, and not topped up above **13%**.
- A name **>40% above its 30W EMA** may be held but **never added to** (mean-reversion risk); >45% is a trim candidate on the next cycle even absent an EMA break, if it also sits at a full weight.
- Any name with an **unexplained promoter-holding move >10% in one quarter** is capped at **≤4%** pending a public explanation, and **exited** if two further quarters pass with no filing.

**C. Liquidity / venue caps.**
- BSE-only or SME-platform names capped at **5% each** and **20% combined** — these cannot be exited into a falling market.
- Prefer the NSE line where a name is dual-listed.

**D. Shared-factor (correlation) caps.** Names that would draw down together on one macro move are capped as a group, regardless of sector labels:
- Power / electrical / industrial-capex cluster (currently Dynamic Cables, Macpower, Yash Highvoltage): **≤ 28%**.
- Pharma (currently Venus Remedies, Novartis): **≤ 25%**.
- Any future cluster of 3+ names sharing a single demand driver: **≤ 28%**.

**E. Cash buffer.**
- Floor **12%** at all times.
- Raised to **≥ 20%** whenever more than half the equity book is >20% extended above its 30W EMA (the current condition) — i.e. when there is little to buy, hold the cash rather than force weight into extended names.

**F. Governance / red-flag exclusions (hard).** Any name in the ValuePickr screen's `avoid` or `exclude` tier, or with a promoter pledge appearing where there was none, an active SEBI/fraud order, or an auditor resignation/qualification, is excluded outright — not sized down. `highCaution`-tier names may be held only at a reduced 3-4% starter weight with the specific concern named.

**G. What "minimal risk of total loss" means here.** A full wipeout needs ~9 independent going-concern failures at once — not a real scenario. The risks this framework actually manages are (1) a systemic small-cap bear (handled by the EMA stops + cash buffer capping the drawdown near the low teens), and (2) a single-name fraud/governance gap-down (handled by the 12% cap, the liquidity caps, and rule B's promoter-holding trigger). The residual worst realistic single event is a large overnight gap on the biggest position — currently ~7% of capital.
